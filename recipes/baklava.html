Pistachio Baklava

Forty layers of butter-brushed filo, crushed pistachios and rose-water syrup. Once you make your own, you'll never buy it again. The secret is patience and good butter.

Prep40 mins
Cook35 mins
Makes24 pieces
DifficultyIntermediate

Ingredients

Baklava

  • 400g filo pastry (about 20 sheets)
  • 200g unsalted butter, melted and clarified
  • 300g shelled pistachios, coarsely ground
  • 2 tbsp caster sugar
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon

Rose-water syrup

  • 350g caster sugar
  • 200ml water
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • 2 tbsp rose water
  • 1 tbsp orange blossom water

Method

  1. Make the syrup. Dissolve sugar in water over medium heat. Add lemon juice and boil for 10 minutes until it coats the back of a spoon lightly. Remove from heat, add rose water and orange blossom water. Cool completely — it must be cold before using.
  2. Prepare the filling. Mix ground pistachios with sugar and cinnamon. Set aside.
  3. Layer the filo. Preheat oven to 180°C/fan 160°C. Butter a 30×20cm baking tray. Lay one sheet of filo in the tray and brush generously with melted butter. Repeat until you have 10 layers. Spread the pistachio filling evenly over the filo. Layer the remaining filo sheets on top, buttering each one as you go.
  4. Cut before baking. With a very sharp knife, cut the baklava into diamonds or squares all the way through to the base. This is essential — you cannot cut it cleanly after baking.
  5. Bake. Bake for 30–35 minutes until deep golden and crisp. Check from 25 minutes — it can catch quickly.
  6. Add the cold syrup. Remove from the oven and immediately pour the cold syrup evenly over the hot baklava. It will sizzle and absorb. Leave to cool completely in the tray — at least 2 hours — before serving.

Chef's Notes

Cold syrup on hot baklava — not the other way around. This is the non-negotiable rule. Hot syrup makes the pastry soggy.

Baklava keeps at room temperature, loosely covered, for up to a week. Do not refrigerate — it makes the pastry soft.
