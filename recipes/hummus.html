Silky Hummus bi Tahini

The hummus I grew up eating — made properly, with dried chickpeas soaked overnight and an obscene amount of good tahini. The extra effort over tinned chickpeas is worth every minute.

Prep15 mins + overnight soak
Cook45 mins
Serves4–6
DifficultyEasy

Ingredients

  • 300g dried chickpeas (or 2 × 400g tins, drained)
  • 1 tsp bicarbonate of soda (if using dried)
  • 150g good-quality tahini (I use Al Nakhil)
  • 2 large garlic cloves, crushed
  • 60ml freshly squeezed lemon juice (about 2 lemons)
  • 1 tsp fine sea salt, plus more to taste
  • 80–100ml ice-cold water

To serve

  • Good olive oil
  • Pinch of sumac
  • Pinch of smoked paprika
  • Fresh flatbread or crudités

Method

  1. Soak the chickpeas. The night before, cover dried chickpeas with plenty of cold water and leave to soak for at least 12 hours.
  2. Cook until very soft. Drain and rinse the soaked chickpeas. Place in a large saucepan with the bicarbonate of soda and cover with fresh cold water by at least 5cm. Bring to a boil, skimming any foam, then simmer for 45–60 minutes until completely tender — they should crush between your fingers with no resistance. Drain, reserving a mugful of cooking liquid.
  3. Blend the tahini base. In a food processor, blitz the garlic and lemon juice together for 30 seconds. Add the tahini and a pinch of salt and blitz for 1 minute until thick and pale.
  4. Add the chickpeas. With the processor running, add the warm chickpeas in batches. Pour in the ice-cold water gradually — the cold temperature is what makes it turn silky white. Blitz for at least 3–4 minutes, scraping down the sides. Taste and adjust lemon and salt.
  5. Serve.) Spread onto a wide plate with the back of a spoon, making a well in the centre. Drizzle generously with olive oil, dust with sumac and paprika. Eat immediately with warm flatbread.

Chef's Notes

The single biggest upgrade you can make to hummus is to peel the chickpeas after cooking — it takes patience but produces an incredibly smooth result. Rub them gently between two tea towels and the skins slip off easily.

Hummus keeps covered in the fridge for 3 days. Bring back to room temperature and add a splash of water before serving.
