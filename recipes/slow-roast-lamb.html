Slow-Roast Shoulder of Lamb

Seven hours low and slow with cumin, coriander and preserved lemon. The kind of centrepiece that earns silence at the table — the meat falls apart at the lightest touch.

Prep20 mins
Cook7 hrs
Serves6–8
DifficultyEasy

Ingredients

  • 2.5kg bone-in lamb shoulder
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 2 preserved lemon quarters, flesh removed, rind finely chopped
  • 2 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • ½ tsp cayenne pepper
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 tsp flaky sea salt
  • 250ml water or light lamb stock
  • Juice of 1 lemon

Method

  1. Score and stuff. With a small sharp knife, make 12–15 deep incisions all over the lamb shoulder. Push a slice of garlic and a pinch of preserved lemon into each one.
  2. Make the spice paste. Combine cumin, coriander, cinnamon, paprika, cayenne, olive oil and salt into a paste. Rub all over the lamb, working it into every surface. Leave to marinate for at least 1 hour, or overnight in the fridge.
  3. Set up the roasting tray. Preheat the oven to 160°C/fan 140°C. Place the lamb in a large roasting tray. Pour the water or stock around the base. Squeeze the lemon juice over the meat. Cover tightly with two layers of foil, sealing the edges well.
  4. Slow roast. Place in the oven and roast for 6½–7 hours. Do not open the foil — the steam is what makes it fall apart.
  5. Crisp the top. Remove the foil, increase the heat to 220°C and roast for a further 20 minutes until the surface is browned and slightly caramelised.
  6. Rest and serve. Rest the lamb for 20 minutes, then shred it at the table with two forks. Serve with flatbreads, yoghurt and a herb salad.

Chef's Notes

This is one of those rare dishes where doing less produces a better result. Seal the foil tightly, trust the oven and walk away.

The cooking juices left in the tray are liquid gold — skim the fat, reduce by half in a saucepan and serve as a sauce alongside.
