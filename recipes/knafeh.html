Knafeh with Orange Blossom Syrup

Shredded pastry pressed over warm, stretchy cheese and soaked in fragrant sugar syrup — a dessert that stops conversation at the table. Best eaten immediately, while the cheese is still molten.

Prep20 mins
Cook30 mins
Serves8
DifficultyIntermediate

Ingredients

Knafeh

  • 400g kataifi pastry (shredded filo), thawed
  • 150g unsalted butter, melted
  • 500g fresh mozzarella or akkawi cheese, sliced
  • 250g ricotta
  • 2 tbsp semolina
  • Pinch of fine sea salt

Orange blossom syrup

  • 300g caster sugar
  • 200ml water
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • 2 tbsp orange blossom water
  • 1 tsp rose water

To finish

  • 3 tbsp crushed pistachios

Method

  1. Make the syrup first. Combine sugar and water in a saucepan over medium heat, stirring until dissolved. Add lemon juice and bring to a boil. Simmer for 8 minutes until slightly thickened. Remove from heat, stir in orange blossom water and rose water. Set aside to cool.
  2. Prepare the pastry. Roughly chop the kataifi pastry into 3–4cm lengths. Pour the melted butter over it and use your hands to coat every strand evenly.
  3. Prepare the cheese. If using akkawi, soak in cold water for 2 hours to remove excess salt, then drain and pat dry. Mix the cheese with ricotta, semolina and a pinch of salt.
  4. Assemble. Preheat oven to 200°C/fan 180°C. Press half the buttered kataifi into a well-buttered 30cm round baking dish or oven-proof frying pan, forming an even, compact layer. Spread the cheese mixture evenly on top. Cover with the remaining kataifi, pressing firmly.
  5. Bake. Bake for 25–30 minutes until deep golden and crisp all over. Remove from the oven.
  6. Flip and syrup. Place a large plate or board over the pan and carefully invert the knafeh so the golden side faces up. Immediately pour the cooled syrup generously over the top (use at least half — it absorbs quickly). Scatter crushed pistachios over the surface and serve at once.

Chef's Notes

The syrup must be cold when it hits the hot knafeh — this is the rule. Hot syrup on hot pastry makes it soggy. Make the syrup well ahead and refrigerate it.

Kataifi pastry is available in Middle Eastern and Greek food shops, often in the frozen section.
